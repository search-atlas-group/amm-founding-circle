# Lanes Momentum Framework

**Purpose:** give an agency a short-horizon decision surface between long-range planning and daily execution.

## The doctrine

A lane is a meaningful outcome or project, not a task dump. Rank lanes left-to-right by current business impact, keep three or four active lanes by default, and make the first week specific. Weeks two and three are lighter; everything else stays in Beyond until it earns attention.

Every lane has one accountable human owner. The commander supplies the mission, context, prioritization, and final refinement; assigning an agent does not remove human accountability.

## Board grammar

- White: not started.
- Yellow: active.
- Red: blocked or requiring a decision.
- Green: complete; move it to the done shelf.

Cards should be short and evidence-linked. A board is not a diary, ticket dump, or unexplained link wall.

## Daily lane walk

1. Start at the highest-impact lane.
2. Confirm what changed.
3. Move completed cards to the done shelf.
4. Resolve blockers by unblocking, narrowing, reassigning, deferring, or stopping.
5. Recommit the next card and its definition of done.
6. Keep only the relevant team in the room; six is the ceiling, four is a useful default.

## Commander test

If the commander owns every card, the system has not created leverage. Coach the owner, clarify the brief, and preserve the commander's attention for judgment. A leader can earn broader ownership by demonstrating outcomes, not by receiving a title first.

## Relationship to other systems

- `quarterly-rocks-tracker` owns quarterly commitments.
- `agency-scorecard` owns recurring metrics.
- This framework owns the three-week initiative field and daily movement.
- `dispatch-and-brief` and `thread-to-spec` own detailed execution briefs.
