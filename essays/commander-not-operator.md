# Commander, not operator

The founder's job changes when the team can execute. It is not to touch every card, rescue every agent, or keep every decision in one head. It is to decide what matters, prepare the mission, give one person ownership, and return for the review that protects quality.

That change begins before execution. A useful brief names the outcome, the context, the source of truth, the boundaries, and what “done” can be observed. Without that preparation, delegation is merely the transfer of confusion.

The commander does not disappear. The commander supplies the final contribution: the priority, the correction, the tradeoff, the encouragement, and the decision to stop or continue. But the commander should not remain the owner of every lane. If no one else can own a meaningful outcome, the system has preserved a bottleneck rather than built a team.

A lane gives a person a testable opportunity. The owner has one outcome, a short horizon, visible evidence, and a daily place to report drift. When a card is blocked, the question is not “why did you fail?” but “what decision, context, or scope is missing?” Coach first. Then inspect the result.

This is also how agentic work stays honest. An agent may execute a broad mission, but a human still owns the outcome and the external write. The commander leaves the session after giving the agent a complete brief, then reviews the artifact against the definition of done. Attention is spent on judgment, not on watching a spinner.

The framework is not a promise that every person will immediately become a leader. It is a way to discover who can own work, what support is missing, and where the operating system is still too dependent on the founder.
