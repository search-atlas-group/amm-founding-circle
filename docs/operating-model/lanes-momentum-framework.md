# Lanes Momentum Framework

A lightweight operating system for prioritization, focus, ownership, and momentum.

## The model

A **lane** is a meaningful outcome or project that normally spans at least several weeks. It is not a task, diary, ticket dump, or folder of links. Cards inside a lane are the short steps that move the outcome forward.

The commander designs the board and keeps the team aligned. The owner of the lane is accountable for landing the outcome. Supporters may contribute, but the lane has one accountable owner.

## Board shape

| Horizon | Meaning | Density |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Work that must become real now | Densest and specific |
| Week 2 | The next likely sequence | About half as dense |
| Week 3 | Near-term follow-on | About half as dense |
| Beyond | Important, but not yet in the active field | Sparse / held |
| Done shelf | Completed outcomes and cards | Visible evidence of momentum |

Keep three or four active lanes by default. A larger crisis or company-wide initiative may temporarily require more, but that is an exception to name explicitly. Plan no more than three weeks in detail; the future is allowed to change.

## Card status

- **White:** not started.
- **Yellow:** in work.
- **Red:** blocked or missed commitment requiring a decision.
- **Green:** completed; move it to the done shelf rather than leaving it on the active board.

Card text should be short enough to compress context. Put the detail in the linked brief or artifact, not in a wall of prose on the board.

## Lane walk

Run a daily lane walk with the smallest relevant group. Move left to right, then down the lane:

1. Reconfirm the top-left priority.
2. Ask the owner what changed since the last walk.
3. Mark completed cards green and move them to the done shelf.
4. Identify red cards: blocked, late, or under-scoped.
5. Make one decision: unblock, reassign, narrow, defer, or stop.
6. Recommit the next card and its definition of done.

Do not use a weekly meeting as the first time the team discovers it drifted. Keep a working meeting to six people or fewer; four is a useful default.

## Commander rules

1. Do the thought work before assigning execution.
2. Put the outcome, context, source of truth, and definition of done in a brief.
3. Do not own every lane. Preserve attention for prioritization and the final 10%.
4. Give people a meaningful lane before deciding they cannot own one.
5. Use agents as autonomous executors, not as chat-based errand runners.
6. Review output against the definition of done; do not confuse activity with completion.
7. Celebrate green lanes and the people who moved them.

## Mission brief minimum

Every lane should link to a brief containing:

- Outcome and why it matters.
- Owner and supporting roles.
- Source systems and access assumptions.
- Current state and known constraints.
- Scope and explicit out-of-scope.
- Week-by-week sequence.
- Definition of done.
- Review / write gate.
- Failure and escalation path.

## Anti-patterns

- **Diary:** a dated log of everything someone did.
- **Task dump:** hundreds of tickets with no prioritization.
- **Artifact wall:** links without the interpretation needed to act.
- **Shared ownership:** several people accountable, so nobody is.
- **Overloaded founder:** every lane carries the commander’s name.
- **Overbuilt horizon:** weeks two and three are planned with false precision.
