## Chapters

### 01. Opening and agenda

Evidence: `i=0–18`.

The room names the day's intended program and recaps earlier teaching. Announced offer, StoryBrand and profit sessions are not evidence that those sessions occurred in this source.

**Implement:**

1. Record the actual business outcome to pursue.
2. Keep announced-but-uncovered topics outside the implementation scope.

### 02. Autonomous work and a shared service-skills repository

Evidence: `i=19–42`.

Give agents a complete objective rather than babysitting. Maintain one team skills repository, document the client onboarding process, and prove one end-to-end run.

**Implement:**

1. Choose one service the agency already sells.
2. Map its inputs, owner, skills and review gates.
3. Run one authorized pilot and attach evidence before rollout.

Resource: [playbooks/service-skill-rollout.md](../../playbooks/service-skill-rollout.md).

### 03. Member takeaways: paid media, delivery and sales readiness

Evidence: `i=43–118`.

The discussion surfaces volatility in paid media, the need for a capable team, contracts, and pre-meeting preparation. These are participant observations, not independently verified performance findings.

**Implement:**

1. List the current delivery and lead-handling gaps.
2. Verify account-specific evidence before changing campaigns.
3. Route contracts and compensation decisions through qualified human review.

### 04. Front-load the thinking and prepare the mission

Evidence: `i=119–175`.

Prepare context, scope and the definition of done before delegating. The same operating framework can be reused for different objectives.

**Implement:**

1. Write the outcome, sources, constraints and exclusions.
2. Name the required artifact and proof of completion.
3. Specify when the agent must stop for a decision.

Resource: [templates/mission-brief-template.md](../../templates/mission-brief-template.md).

### 05. From founder execution to team ownership

Evidence: `i=176–243`.

Member reflections lead into the need for people who own outcomes rather than leaving the founder as the only operator. Personal anecdotes and private business details are intentionally omitted.

**Implement:**

1. Identify one founder-dependent process.
2. Select a human owner and coaching plan.
3. Keep strategy and review distinct from doing every task.

### 06. Lanes, priorities and the done shelf

Evidence: `i=244–303`.

Introduce the Lanes Momentum Framework: meaningful outcome lanes, left-to-right priority, status colors, a visible done shelf and denser near-term planning.

**Implement:**

1. Choose three or four meaningful outcomes.
2. Put the most important lane at the left.
3. Use white/not-started, yellow/working, red/blocked and green/done.
4. Move completed cards to a done shelf with evidence.

Resource: [templates/lanes-board-template.md](../../templates/lanes-board-template.md).

### 07. Board mistakes, capacity and exactly one owner

Evidence: `i=304–368`.

Avoid diary boards, task dumps and artifact walls. Keep the planning horizon to three weeks and exactly one accountable owner per lane.

**Implement:**

1. Remove unnecessary dates and lengthy card text.
2. Use annotated evidence where it clarifies the decision.
3. Check capacity instead of assigning every lane to one person.
4. Leave later weeks lighter and excess work in Beyond.

Resource: [skills/lanes-momentum-framework/SKILL.md](../../skills/lanes-momentum-framework/SKILL.md).

### 08. Worked examples and the daily lane walk

Evidence: `i=369–406`.

Team examples illustrate one owner, iterative commander feedback, priority and a daily walk that unblocks, recommits and moves completed work.

**Implement:**

1. Walk the board in priority order.
2. Ask what landed, what is blocked and what support is needed.
3. Update the next commitment and record the decision.

### 09. Build the first three lanes

Evidence: `i=407–436`.

Members are asked to build their first three lanes using a familiar shared whiteboard. Constructing the board is itself a clarity exercise.

**Implement:**

1. Brain-dump possible work.
2. Group work into outcomes rather than tool names.
3. Build the first three lanes in a team-accessible board.

### 10. Diagnose the constraint before accelerating growth

Evidence: `i=437–490`.

The live exercise separates fulfillment, visibility, lead generation and sales. A case-study engine turns evidenced outcomes into permissioned collateral.

**Implement:**

1. Identify the current delivery bottleneck.
2. Map which services are active and whether results are visible.
3. Prioritize fulfillment capacity before multiplying demand.
4. Collect proof and permission before drafting case studies.

Resource: [skills/case-study-engine/SKILL.md](../../skills/case-study-engine/SKILL.md).

### 11. Assign the command-center and sales work

Evidence: `i=491–563`.

Map the customer journey and separate fulfillment, revenue and meeting context. Assign human ownership and defer lanes that overload the commander. Shared material does not infer participant identities from unreliable diarization.

**Implement:**

1. Assign one owner per workstream and confirm capacity.
2. Separate data-context owners from the build owner.
3. Define read-first source fields and unknowns.
4. Defer work that cannot be staffed.

Resource: [skills/agency-command-center/SKILL.md](../../skills/agency-command-center/SKILL.md).

Evidence: `i=564–595`.

**Implement:**

2. Map each field to its source, owner and freshness.

### 13. Audit the four-stage sales journey

Evidence: `i=596–623`.

Audit website-to-lead, pre-meeting, meeting and post-meeting follow-up. Rank repairs using supplied data and compare the analysis with human judgment. A referenced prior sales transcript is not supplied by this source.

**Implement:**

1. Collect the missing sales context and permitted sources.
2. Map the four stages and define denominators.
3. Report missing evidence instead of fabricating conversion rates.
4. Rank the repair opportunities and await approval.

Resource: [skills/sales-journey-audit/SKILL.md](../../skills/sales-journey-audit/SKILL.md).

### 14. Referrals, expansion and coaching new lane owners

Evidence: `i=624–665`.

Referral and expansion conversations follow real delivered value and a suitable client relationship. Coach potential owners instead of assuming the founder must carry every lane.

**Implement:**

1. Verify the result before proposing a referral or expansion conversation.
2. Treat sentiment as an uncertain input, not permission to send.
3. Give a potential lane owner context and coaching.
4. Measure their outcome and revise capacity.

### 15. Daily realignment and developing team leaders

Evidence: `i=666–712`.

A daily lane walk provides correction and energy. Leaders can emerge through evidenced ownership, and completed lanes make room for the next outcome. No reliable calendar date is inferred for a future commitment.

**Implement:**

1. Set a daily relevant-team walk.
2. Reprioritize and unblock before drift accumulates.
3. Celebrate actual completed outcomes.
4. Coach another person to maintain the board.

### 16. Service-skill mapping and an approval-gated SEO pilot

Evidence: `i=713–750`.

Study the supplied SEO pack, map skills to service buckets, plan and dry-run an authorized pilot, then review before execution. Do not publish content or press releases from a planning request.

**Implement:**

1. Inventory available skills and gaps.
2. Verify client identity, scope and read access.
3. Produce drafts and a mission plan without external writes.
4. Review with the fulfillment owner.
5. Obtain separate execution approval and only then expand.

Resource: [docs/operating-model/seo-fulfillment-implementation-mission-plan.md](../../docs/operating-model/seo-fulfillment-implementation-mission-plan.md).

### 17. Relevant small teams, shared boards and the next commitment

Evidence: `i=751–778`.

Separate meetings when teams differ. Keep meetings small, have participants maintain the board, and export a source board screenshot rather than inventing one.

**Implement:**

1. Include only people relevant to the board.
2. Aim for four participants and do not exceed six.
3. Confirm the first three lanes and owners.
4. Save the actual board screenshot privately and link its evidence.
