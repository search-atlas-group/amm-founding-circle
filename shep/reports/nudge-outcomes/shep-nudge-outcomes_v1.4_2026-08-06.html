The short version

what the day actually shows

Shep watched 100 agent panes for 13.6 hours and delivered 214 nudges to 58 of them. Most of the machinery worked: the model drafted 356 times, correctly kept quiet 144 of those, and never once wrote an over-long instruction.

But the nudges did not carry sessions over the finish line. Only 9 of 44 nudged panes reached a clean end state in the window. The single biggest reason is the closing prompt: of 73 times shep asked a session to declare itself safe to close, only 5 were followed by an actual close, and 17 panes had to be asked more than once — one of them 6 times.

The second reason is staleness: 105 of 145 recommendations (72%) went out of date before anyone could send them.

Asks opening with a bare SAFE_TO_CLOSE17shep's own words echo back as a false close

Three separate faults stack up here:

  1. Shep could not tell it was repeating itself. Every close ask carries a reason written fresh from whatever is on screen, so two asks minutes apart shared few words — a median of 0.39 similarity against the 0.70 duplicate threshold. The guard caught 6 of 34 repeats on wording alone; the rest read as new, because the guard compares words and the words genuinely differed.
  2. The ask was too long. Every one of the 73 close asks ran over 80 characters, median 132. A closing instruction has to be the shortest thing on screen, not the longest.
  3. 17 asks opened with the literal token. When shep types SAFE_TO_CLOSE as the first thing in the pane, its own echo can satisfy the close detector — a close signal made of our words, not the agent's.

Automatic sends are invisible to the scorecard

one open defect
214 vs 62
nudges actually delivered vs nudges the outcome log recorded

Only the 62 operator-approved sends write a sent event. The 152 automatic continuations do not, so the outcome scorecard under-counts delivery by roughly 3.5x and cannot attribute anything that followed them. Every send-side number on this page therefore comes from the action log, which is complete.

Two telemetry bugs, now fixed

verified with regression tests
2547
“progress” events recorded for 214 nudges — before the fix

1. Shep credited itself. When a nudge is typed into a pane the text echoes back. The guard that ignores that echo compared the pane with whitespace collapsed, but the terminal hard-wraps a long nudge mid-word — so the echoed text was never found verbatim and shep read its own typing as the agent making progress.

2. One nudge was credited many times. Progress was recorded on every polling frame rather than once per send, so a single burst of work produced dozens of success events.

Together these inflated the record to 2547 progress events for 214 nudges. Both are fixed and pinned with regression tests, so tomorrow's numbers will mean what they say. The counts on this page deliberately avoid the progress signal entirely.
